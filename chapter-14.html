Module 14 · Chapter 14

Threat Intelligence, YARA & Detection Engineering

Threat intelligence turns individual observations into reusable detections. Good detection engineering emphasizes durable behavior and context rather than brittle indicators alone.

14.1 Indicators

Hashes, domains, IP addresses, filenames, registry paths, and certificates can be useful, but they change. Behavioral and contextual detections often remain valuable longer.

14.2 YARA

YARA rules can classify files or memory content based on patterns. Build rules from stable characteristics and test them against both known positives and representative benign samples.

14.3 Sigma and Log Detection

Sigma-style detections express log-based behavior in a platform-neutral form. Focus on meaningful combinations such as suspicious parent-child relationships, unusual command interpreters, or unexpected persistence changes.

14.4 Detection Lifecycle

  1. Define the threat hypothesis.
  2. Identify available telemetry.
  3. Write a minimal detection.
  4. Test against benign activity.
  5. Measure false positives.
  6. Add context and tuning.
  7. Document and review periodically.
